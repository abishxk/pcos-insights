About this estimate

Here is what each part of your result means.

1
The big number
We line your answers up against the {{ meta.n_samples }} people in our data and take the {{ meta.best_params.n_neighbors }} most similar. The number is how many of those had PCOS, so 60% means roughly 6 in 10 of the people most like you had it. It is not a 60% chance that you have it.
2
Low, medium or high
Your number placed against the base rate: {{ base_rate }} in 100 people in this data had PCOS. That is the line every estimate is compared to.
3
The signs you share
Which of your answers turn up more often in people who have PCOS, and how much more often. A sign that is 2x more common appears twice as often in that group.
4
What to do next
Whether this result is worth taking to a doctor, and how accurate the tool was when tested on patients it had never seen before.
